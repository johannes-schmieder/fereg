This checkpoint preserves 250 application attempts: 244 successful calls, two
allocation rejections, two timeouts and two explicit-LSMR warm-up failures.
It records kernel experiments, including rejected guards, rather than admitting
a new automatic routing policy or completing the September 21 milestone.
Nothing was installed, pushed or qualified on another platform.

## Memory diagnostics and remaining work

Both candidates reject at 300 MB usable with r(909). At 1.5 GB usable both time
out after sixty seconds; those failures remain open. Both complete and pass
independent science with a finite 4 GB usable cap. Observed tracked allocation
peaks remain below that cap.

A tiny diagnostic linked to the tested core explains the routing pressure.
For N=245,760, 52 RHS columns, 81,920/2,048 levels and one worker, modeled LSMR
workspace is 3,489,835,280 bytes with Schwarz, 1,143,472,656 with diagonal and
967,475,728 with no preconditioner, before the execution parent. These are
unchanged model outputs, not proposed smaller bounds.

Explicit diagonal/off LSMR fail their warm-up certificates and never invoke
the target. Using those existing options with auto's stricter recurrence
target completes the target at 2/1.5 GB usable. Both pass independent inference
and output checks, with tracked peaks 635,632,167/634,960,407 bytes. These traced,
unpaired diagnostics take 46.337/54.019 seconds; they are not headline timing
comparisons or routing acceptance. Every rejection, timeout and warm-up failure
is retained alongside the four successful memory-science checks.

The current batch transport leaves up to 64 RHS columns unwindowed, including
serial fits. Explicitly bounding live RHS work is a subsequent experiment; no
memory model or preconditioner policy was changed in this checkpoint. Broader
controls, final independent blocks, automatic-route calibration and Linux
confirmation remain open. SCC authentication still fails before inspection or
submission.
